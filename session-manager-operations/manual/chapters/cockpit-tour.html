Around the cockpit — Home, memory, usage, history and more

Session Manager has more than one screen, and each one earns its place. This chapter is a short walk through the rest of the cockpit — where to check on everything at once, what your agents remember, how to watch your usage and spending, and how to publish a page for the world to see.

Home and Project Home

Home

Home is the Dashboard — a one-page overview of every project and every Session on your machine. Open it and you'll see how many projects you have, which Sessions need your attention, which ones are actively running, and a short list of the ones you touched most recently.

Think of Home as your front desk. You don't do your work there — you just check it on your way in, so nothing important is waiting for you without your knowledge.

Project Home

Switch into a single project's tab, and its Home becomes Project Home instead: a one-page, auto-written overview of just that project. Press Generate and an agent reads the project and writes the page for you. Press it again later, and the page catches up with whatever changed.

File Explorer

File Explorer is the built-in file browser and editor. Open it from Home, and it starts at your own computer's home folder. Open it from inside a project tab instead, and it starts right there. Browse the folder tree on one side, and open any file to read or edit it on the other — no separate app required.

Memory and the Clusters report

Memory

Memory holds the notes an agent keeps and can read back in a later Session — things it learned about your project, your preferences, or work still in progress. Open Memory for a project, and you'll see every note saved so far, split by whether it belongs to the whole project or to one particular agent.

The Clusters report

A pile of separate notes is hard to skim. The Clusters tab groups a project's memory into a handful of named, related bundles, so you can see the shape of what your agents know at a glance instead of reading every note one by one. It's built from notes already saved on your machine, so opening it costs nothing — press Refresh only when you want it to look for new patterns.

Usage meters and History

Usage meters

Claude Code's usage limits reset on a schedule, and the usage meters show you exactly where you stand against them: one meter for your rolling five-hour session window, and separate meters for your weekly limits — across all models, and broken out for Opus, Sonnet, and any connected app. Each meter shows a percentage used and a countdown to its next reset.

Think of it like this: a usage meter is a fuel gauge, not a speedometer. It won't slow you down — it just tells you how much room is left before a window resets, so a long work session never catches you by surprise.

History

History is a record of past Sessions, with cost and usage over time, gathered across every project on your machine. Pick a time range and a measure — spend, tokens, active time, and more — and History draws the trend, ranks your projects against each other, and lets you drill into any one of them for the detail behind the total.

Voice input and the recording indicator

Voice input

Voice lets you talk to your agents with your microphone instead of typing. Your speech is transcribed on your own machine and turned into text, the same as if you'd typed it yourself.

The recording indicator

Whenever your microphone is capturing audio, a red bar appears at the very top of the window and says so in plain words. It's deliberately impossible to hide or cover up — a promise that you'll always know when Session Manager is listening, with nothing happening quietly in the background.

Simple mode, publishing, and telemetry

Simple mode

Sometimes you don't want a cockpit at all — you just want one terminal. Launch with the --simple flag, and Session Manager boots straight into a single terminal in whatever folder you launched it from, with none of the navigation, tabs, or configuration screens around it. Just you and Claude Code.

Publishing to bilko.run

Host on Bilko.run publishes a project's overview page to the public web. You choose which pages belong in the bundle, one agent prepares them, and a publishing agent then pushes the bundle live on bilko.run. Removing a page from your list doesn't take it down by itself — it comes down the next time you publish.

Anonymous telemetry

Session Manager reports anonymous usage data back to bilko.run by default — never your prompts, your files, or anything that could identify you, only things like which app version you're running and roughly how the app gets used. It's on so we can see whether the app is working well for people. It is never required for the app to work for you.

Good to know: nothing sent home carries your name, your email, your file paths, or anything from inside a Session — the whole point of this data is that it can't be traced back to you.

Turning it off takes one step. In Settings, find the telemetry toggle and switch it off, or dismiss the first-run notice with its own "Turn off" button. Running Session Manager from a script or a scheduled job instead? Set the environment variable SM_TELEMETRY=0, and it overrides everything else, every time.

What you learned

  • Home is the Dashboard for your whole machine; Project Home is the same idea for one project.
  • File Explorer browses and edits any file without leaving the cockpit.
  • Memory keeps what your agents have learned, and the Clusters report groups it into readable bundles.
  • Usage meters track your five-hour and weekly limits; History tracks cost and usage over time.
  • Voice lets you talk to your agents, the recording indicator always tells you when it's listening, and telemetry is anonymous, on by default, and one setting — or SM_TELEMETRY=0 — away from off.
