Around the cockpit — Home, memory, usage, history and more

Session Manager has more than one screen, and each one earns its place. This chapter is a short walk through the rest of the cockpit — where to check on everything at once, what your agents remember, how to watch your usage and spending, and how to publish a page for the world to see.

Home and Project Home

Home

Home is the Guild Hall — a small 2D world where every active Session is a character, grouped by agent type. A bar on top says how many slots are busy, and carries Refresh and the pause-above control, which holds new work once your usage passes the level you set.

The Guild Hall shows the situation, and it lets you answer questions. Open the Quest board for your Needs you list: tool confirmations, where background work asks to use a risky tool, and questions sent by outside apps. Everything else happens on its own page. To approve a Session, retry or adopt work, or nudge the Scheduler, click the row's button: Open session, View in Scheduler, or Open for an outside app's link.

Home re-reads everything when you open it, at most every 30 seconds. Press Refresh for a fresher look; a stamp beside it says when it last updated.

Think of Home as your front desk. You check it on your way in, so nothing waits for you unseen.

Project Home

Switch into a single project's tab, and its Home becomes Project Home instead: an auto-written overview of just that project. Four buttons switch the area below: Pages for written overviews, Videos for short demo videos, Tools, and Feedback. Each press makes one new entry you can rename or delete. Inside Videos, the Voices view lets you Play each narrator voice first.

Under Videos, a small Scripts section holds one switch per project: Scripts may run Demo Video. It starts off. Turn it on, or ask an agent in chat to turn it on for you, and that project's own scripts (say, the one that publishes your blog) may make a Demo Video by themselves. Each one still opens a normal session you can see, and a video with Review first still waits for your approval before it is saved.

A session a script started is tagged Started by script in the Sessions list and in the session header, with the script's name when it gave one. A session you start yourself by pressing a button shows no such tag.

Feedback from your website

The Feedback tab collects bug reports from visitors to your own website. Under Website widget, list your sites and press Create widget, then Copy snippet and paste that line into your site's HTML. Visitors mark up a screenshot and send a note, which lands in the Inbox. A report's Open as Epic button turns it into a Proposed Session (Epic is the app's code name for one).

Reports travel through the bilko.run relay, so pair this computer under App Settings, then Web remote, first.

Want to chat? Book a free consultation. That line shows in three places. A quiet line above the footer shows it, and you can dismiss it. It comes back at most once per version. The Feedback panel has the same link. So does the Help menu item Book a Free Consultation. The first conversation is free.

File Explorer

File Explorer is the built-in file browser and editor. Open it from Home, and it starts at your home folder; open it inside a project tab, and it starts there. Pick a file in the tree to read or edit it.

Inside a project, Artifacts lists files your agents saved for you to review, such as a video or a report. Video and audio play right in the pane. A menu above the tree also opens a Session's unmerged copy of your project, read-only; pick Project to go back.

Memory and the Clusters report

Memory

Memory holds the notes an agent keeps and can read back in a later Session — things it learned about your project, your preferences, or work still in progress. Inside a project's tab, open Claude config from the sidebar; its tabs are listed down the page's left rail. The Memory tab shows every note saved so far, split by whether it belongs to the whole project or to one particular agent.

The Clusters report

A pile of separate notes is hard to skim. The Clusters tab groups a project's memory into a few named bundles, so you see the shape of what your agents know at a glance. Opening it costs nothing; press Refresh only when you want it to look for new patterns.

Usage meters and History

Usage meters

Claude Code's usage limits reset on a schedule, and the usage meters show where you stand: one meter for your rolling five-hour window, and others for your weekly limits, across all models and broken out for Opus, Sonnet, and any connected app. Each shows a percentage and a countdown to its reset.

Think of it like this: a usage meter is a fuel gauge, not a speedometer. It won't slow you down — it just tells you how much room is left before a window resets, so a long work session never catches you by surprise.

History

History is a record of past Sessions, with cost and usage over time, gathered across every project on your machine. Pick a time range and a measure — spend, tokens, active time, and more — and History draws the trend, ranks your projects against each other, and lets you drill into any one of them for the detail behind the total.

Near the bottom, the Validation quality panel shows how well the checking works in each project. It shows how often the validator verified work on its first look, how often it refuted work, and how many rounds of fixes a plan needed. It also counts escapes: work the validator passed that later needed a fix anyway.

Voice input and the recording indicator

Voice input

Voice lets you talk to your agents with your microphone instead of typing. Your speech is transcribed on your own machine and turned into text, the same as if you'd typed it yourself. Its settings live on Home, in App Settings, on the Voice tab.

The speech model only loads when you first press the microphone, so that first press takes a few seconds while the voice panel says Loading speech model. After half an hour without using voice, Session Manager unloads it to free memory, and the next press loads it again.

The recording indicator

Whenever your microphone is capturing audio, a red bar appears at the very top of the window and says so in plain words. It's deliberately impossible to hide or cover up — a promise that you'll always know when Session Manager is listening, with nothing happening quietly in the background.

Simple mode, startup and telemetry

Simple mode

Want just one terminal? Launch with the --simple flag, and Session Manager boots straight into a single terminal in the folder you launched it from, with no tabs or screens around it.

Start at login

Want Session Manager open the moment you sign in to your computer? On Home, open App Settings and its Startup tab, and turn on Start at login. Your computer then starts the app for you each time you log in. A line under the switch says what it will start, and tells you if your system can't do this.

Anonymous telemetry

Session Manager reports anonymous usage data back to bilko.run by default — never your prompts or files, only things like which app version you run. It helps us see whether the app works well. The app never needs it to work for you.

Good to know: nothing sent home carries your name, your email, your file paths, or anything from inside a Session — the whole point of this data is that it can't be traced back to you.

Turning it off takes one step. On Home, open App Settings and its Telemetry tab, and switch it off — or press "Turn off" on the first-run notice. From a script, set the environment variable SM_TELEMETRY=0; it overrides everything else.

The web remote: drive all your computers

You can drive Session Manager from a phone or any browser, across several paired computers. Pair each computer once, then pick one from the web app's header. Everything travels through the bilko.run relay, scrambled so only your browser and your computer can read it.

  1. On the computer, install and open Session Manager. Go to App Settings, then Web remote, and turn on Allow remote control from the web.
  2. To let the browser type commands, such as running hostname in a terminal, also turn on Allow command writes. Leave it off if you only want to watch.
  3. On any phone or browser, open bilko.run/projects/session-manager and sign in. Tap Generate pairing code. Back on the computer, choose Pair Device… and enter the 8-character code.
  4. The first time a browser connects to a computer, the web app waits for you to confirm a 6-digit code on that computer (App Settings, then Web remote). Confirm it once per browser, per computer.
  5. To add another computer, repeat steps 1 to 4 on it. To switch, use the computer switcher in the web app header and pick an online computer.
Good to know: the 6-digit code stops anyone in the middle from posing as your computer. You can forget a paired computer, or turn off its command writes, at any time.

Below that, Services lets another app, such as Kiln, start work in one project. Press Pair a service…, pick its project and tools, run the command it gives you, and confirm the matching code. A service on the same machine can instead be paired by an agent with service pair-local, and shows as paired locally. You can revoke it at any time. Work a service opens arrives as a proposed Session that waits for you, unless you set its May start work it opens control to All projects or Selected projects (it starts Off). Work it may start begins on its own, and the Session row says Auto-started by service with the service's name. Create projects is off unless you tick it; when you do, the dialog asks which folder new projects go in.

What you learned

  • Home is the Guild Hall for your whole machine; Project Home is the same idea for one project, with Pages, Videos, Tools, and Feedback from your own website.
  • File Explorer browses and edits any file, and shows the Artifacts your agents saved for review.
  • Memory keeps what your agents have learned, and the Clusters report groups it into readable bundles.
  • Usage meters track your five-hour and weekly limits; History tracks cost and usage over time.
  • Voice lets you talk to your agents, the recording indicator always tells you when it's listening, and telemetry is anonymous, on by default, and one setting — or SM_TELEMETRY=0 — away from off.
  • The web remote lets a phone or browser drive every computer you pair; pair each once, confirm the 6-digit code, then switch from the header.
